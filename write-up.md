Methodology

The Hyperliquid trading dataset was merged with the Bitcoin Fear & Greed Index using daily dates. After cleaning and aligning the data, several trading metrics were created, including daily PnL, win rate, average position size, trade frequency, and long/short behavior.

Trader segmentation was performed to compare:

Frequent vs Infrequent traders
Consistent Winners vs Inconsistent traders

Since leverage information was not available in the dataset, position size (Size USD) was used as a proxy for trading risk.

Key Insights
Insight 1

Extreme Greed periods showed the highest average PnL and win rate.

Insight 2

Traders were most active during Extreme Fear periods, likely due to increased market volatility.

Insight 3

Infrequent traders achieved higher profits per trade than frequent traders, highlighting the importance of trade quality over quantity

Strategy Recommendations
Strategy 1

Consider increasing exposure during Extreme Greed periods, as profitability and win rates were highest.

Strategy 2

Avoid excessive trading during Fear periods, since trading activity rises sharply and may lead to impulsive decisions.

Conclusion

The analysis demonstrates that market sentiment influences both trader behavior and trading performance. Positive sentiment is associated with stronger profitability, while negative sentiment leads to increased trading activity and risk-taking behavior. These findings suggest that sentiment indicators can provide valuable context for trading and risk management decisions.
